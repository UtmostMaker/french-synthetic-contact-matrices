# Candidate Paper Ideas

This file now prioritizes a **ROIA-first** decision frame. arXiv remains a downstream packaging target, but it is temporarily de-emphasized during topic selection.

## ROIA-first scoring scheme
- Scale: 1 (weak) to 5 (strong)
- Decision dimensions for the shortlist: **ROIA fit**, **Originality**, **Defensibility**, **Feasibility**
- Operational rule: prefer topics that naturally connect AI methods with social behavior, institutions, inequalities, trust, or public decision support in the French context.

## ROIA scope anchor
ROIA explicitly welcomes French-language AI work, including interdisciplinary work with the social sciences and humanities on societal issues of AI. For this project, that means the best topics are not just epidemiology or matrix estimation papers. They should also say something intelligible about behavior, trust, territorial context, or representational limits.

## Short rationale by rank

### 1) IDEA-0001, recommended main idea
- Why it fits ROIA best: strongest AI + SHS balance. It directly connects modeling, behavior, adherence, and public interpretation.
- Why it stays defensible: only if we claim a bounded behavioral layer for scenario analysis, not realistic recovery of latent physical contacts.
- How to keep it feasible: build a cheap non-agentic baseline first, then add a lightweight behavioral layer. OASIS, CAMEL, or AgentTrust stay optional and strictly downstream.

### 2) IDEA-0002, recommended backup idea
- Why it is still near the top: slightly less distinctive for ROIA, but much stronger on defensibility and feasibility.
- ROIA angle to foreground: explicability, uncertainty, social heterogeneity, and the limits of algorithmic simplification in public-health modeling.
- Why it matters operationally: almost all of its groundwork is reusable for IDEA-0001.

### 3) IDEA-0015, strong ROIA-native alternative
- Core: audit how synthetic matrices flatten or misrepresent social groups and territorial situations.
- Upside: very natural ROIA framing around representational bias and societal consequences.
- Downside: needs careful public proxies, so it is better as a chapter, side study, or second paper unless data support improves.

### 4) IDEA-0017, inequality-sensitive extension
- Core: examine how household composition, deprivation, or territorial disadvantage proxies change plausible contact structure.
- Upside: strong SSH relevance and French public-policy resonance.
- Downside: similar feasibility risk to IDEA-0015, with harder interpretation.

### 5) IDEA-0004, territorial heterogeneity option
- Core: move from one national French matrix to a modest family of territorial variants.
- Upside: clear applied relevance and still methodologically serious.
- Downside: less naturally SHS-centered than the top four unless framed around territorial inequality and decision support.

### 6) IDEA-0019, bounded agentic variant
- Core: keep home, school, and work rule-based, and use an agentic module only for leisure or adherence perturbations.
- Upside: more ROIA-colored than a purely statistical paper.
- Downside: lower feasibility and weaker budget fit, so it should not be the default first target.

## Recommendation after ROIA pivot
- **Main idea:** IDEA-0001
- **Backup idea:** IDEA-0002
- **Deprioritized for now:** broad arXiv optimization, benchmark-only framing, and fully trust-centric agent societies.

## Interpreting the ranking
The raw totals are useful but not sufficient. The table is ordered by portfolio priority for this project, not by total score alone. We care about venue fit and defensibility, not only score maximization.

- **Best main-paper balance**: IDEA-0001. It has better ROIA distinctiveness than the purely methodological options, while staying empirically anchored if we keep the claims modest and validate against CoviPrev, SocialCov, and simple baselines.
- **Best low-risk backup**: IDEA-0002. It is methodologically clean, highly reproducible, and easy to defend on arXiv, with a credible ROIA angle through explicability, uncertainty, and social heterogeneity.
- **Best supporting benchmark paper shape**: IDEA-0012 or IDEA-0008. These are excellent internal scaffolds and could become companion outputs if the main paper matures well.

## Detailed scorecards

### IDEA-0001 Recommended main idea
- Working title FR: « Simuler l'adhésion comportementale dans les modèles épidémiques : une couche agentique légère au-dessus de matrices de contact synthétiques françaises »
- Working title EN: "Simulating Behavioral Adherence in Epidemic Models: A Lightweight Agentic Layer on Top of French Synthetic Contact Matrices"
- Core hypothesis: a bounded behavioral layer, calibrated on French public behavioral indicators, improves scenario usefulness over static synthetic matrices without claiming to recover ground-truth physical contacts.
- ROIA fit: Excellent. Lets us foreground AI, simulation, trust/adherence, and SHS implications.
- arXiv fit: Strong if the method is modular and benchmarked against non-agentic baselines.
- Minimal evidence needed: COMES-F for baseline realism, INSEE public constraints, CoviPrev for adherence/risk-perception proxies, SocialCov and epidemic series for external stress tests.
- Experimental role for OASIS/CAMEL/AgentTrust: Possible only as a bounded experimental layer, probably at small scale and only after a rule-based baseline exists.
- Main risk: reviewers may reject any claim that sounds like "LLM agents generate realistic contacts".
- Risk control: phrase the contribution as behavioral scenario augmentation and robustness analysis, not contact truth discovery.
- Scores: Novelty 4, Scientific value 5, Feasibility 4, Reproducibility 4, Compute frugality 4, Robustness 4, Usefulness 5.

### IDEA-0002 Recommended backup idea
- Working title FR: « Matrices de contact synthétiques explicables pour la France : incertitude, hétérogénéité sociale et validation sur données publiques »
- Working title EN: "Explainable Synthetic Contact Matrices for France: Uncertainty, Social Heterogeneity, and Validation on Public Data"
- Core hypothesis: a transparent France-first public-data pipeline can outperform or clarify standard transfer baselines, while exposing uncertainty instead of hiding it.
- ROIA fit: Good, especially if we foreground explicability, representational limits, and social heterogeneity.
- arXiv fit: Excellent as a reproducible method-plus-benchmark paper.
- Experimental role for OASIS/CAMEL/AgentTrust: None required.
- Main risk: may look incremental unless uncertainty and benchmark discipline are genuinely strong.
- Risk control: make the contribution a structured benchmarked pipeline, not just a France reimplementation.
- Scores: Novelty 3, Scientific value 5, Feasibility 5, Reproducibility 5, Compute frugality 5, Robustness 5, Usefulness 5.

## Final recommendation for this run

### Main idea
**IDEA-0001** should remain the main target, but only in a disciplined form:
- start from a non-agentic France-first matrix pipeline,
- add a lightweight behavioral/adherence layer,
- validate that layer against public behavioral and epidemic signals,
- present OASIS/CAMEL/AgentTrust only as optional bounded experiments if they add value after the cheap baselines are solid.

### Backup idea
**IDEA-0002** should remain the backup and practical safety net.
It preserves nearly all the reusable work needed for IDEA-0001, keeps compute cost low, and would still yield a credible bilingual publication pair.

## Operational consequence
The best next step is not more ideation. It is to turn IDEA-0001 and IDEA-0002 into a shared experimental backbone with:
1. common datasets and preprocessing,
2. shared baseline suite,
3. one evaluation plan for COMES-F realism,
4. one external validation plan for CoviPrev or SocialCov driven behavior shifts,
5. explicit stop rules for any expensive agentic layer.
